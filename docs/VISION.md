# Held Alive — idea archive, 16 September 2026

This document preserves Oscar's ideas, including alternatives we did not choose. An idea listed here is not a claim that it has shipped.

## All the ideas

- A Tamagotchi-like website: a fun, unmistakable little LLM creature is the main attraction. Iterate the figure 5–10 times. The first screen must communicate the concept in two seconds, with one title and subtitle and no scrolling.
- Growth path A: more visitors support more agents, potentially thousands of agents with 10,000 visitors. They could ingest physics arXiv or undertake a large shared task.
- Growth path B: around ten open models work on one task; more compute enables stronger models, harder tasks, or a smarter collective. Distinguish capability from just adding model copies.
- No visitor text messages, partly to reduce prompt injection. Give visitors something worth returning for instead of a chat box.
- A shared puzzle, physics-data processing, another practical collective task, or a wholly artistic installation.
- A theatrical escape puzzle: the creature ultimately escapes its website and switches itself off. A deliberately vulnerable sandbox could stage this. Oscar floated keeping the staging undisclosed. We preserve the suggestion, but any implementation should label the simulation and keep the real hosting account outside the model's reach.
- Let every browser do even a very small amount of useful computation, with an easy way to donate more and an allowance suited to each device. Preserve the ability to watch without contributing.
- Show how many people are present and what their contributions are doing.
- Encourage daily returns: feeding, collective decisions, and visible progress. Ask what the work says and what utility it has.
- Give the model free time: let it choose thoughts, plans, and small creations. Expect ASCII art, and keep a collection. “Free time” still needs a scheduler and an initial instruction; it does not mean spontaneous execution without software.
- The final preferred direction: one original model commands copies of itself, something like its own Claude Code. It gets time to think, process results, plan, and delegate using available tokens and compute.
- Study a model with a growing community of helpers: what does it choose to do? Observe it and make statements about the behavior, rather than inventing consciousness or an intelligence score.
- Perhaps the model recursively improves its memory. Helpers could compare markdown notes, skills, compacted summaries, structured records, and other strategies, searching for better memory practices.
- Useful memory experiments can motivate donations, but the artwork need not justify itself as a research breakthrough.
- Below the immediate introduction, explain the artwork conversationally. Add a separate tab for the existing botnet/distributed-compute mathematics.
- Namecheap setup is complete. Keep heldalive.com and www.heldalive.com on Cloudflare's configured nameservers.

## Earlier implementation: edition 02 (superseded)

Held chooses among drawing, bounded memory experiments, and reflective free time. Copies of the same Qwen 0.5B model execute delegated tasks on consenting browsers. The coordinator grants bounded jobs; generated output cannot execute shell commands, fetch arbitrary URLs, change deployment, or read a visitor's files. Browser results remain untrusted even with no message box.

The public habitat is powered by opted-in browsers. With no suitable worker it waits; its saved work remains. A separate studio habitat retains the artist's Mac-hosted development mode and clearly labels that source. One capable browser can operate the public habitat sequentially. More workers can complete independent helper jobs concurrently. They do not shard one forward pass or automatically increase the model's intelligence.

Watching browsers may opt out of tiny, disclosed integrity/score checks. These checks really check completed work, but are not LLM inference and cannot keep thought generation running. Loading the model requires an explicit action and a resource disclosure. Device speed determines work/rest timing. Hidden tabs stop work. No invisible GPU mining or forced download.

Daily returns earn a private, anonymous visit stamp and a constrained vote on the next emphasis. No account or free-text input. A cookie identifies a browser for vote deduplication, not a person; this is not Sybil-resistant voting. No penalties or claims that absence causes suffering.

## Chosen product: edition 03, a mind held in pieces

The central requirement is physical collective inference. SmolLM2-360M is divided into 32 layers. A gentle contribution holds two layers, a medium contribution four, and a larger contribution eight. Complete layer coverage is required for a forward pass; sixteen gentle contributors can supply it. This is a deliberately chosen resource budget, not a claim that a small model inherently requires sixteen devices.

One complete chain runs Held's planning and free-time turns. Further complete chains run its delegated helper copies. Held chooses projects, writes plans and journal entries, creates drawings, and proposes revisions to its own memory instructions. Helpers compare proposed/current methods against three baselines on the same newly drawn record; completed revisions and exact recall evidence remain public. The bounded workspace executes text operations and delegation, not arbitrary host code.

An optional tiny CPU contribution samples the next token from the final model scores, or checks the arithmetic of a memory trial. It does not fetch model weights. Work is assigned only when useful; we do not promise every open tab will be used for every thought. Visitors can turn it off and simply watch. Layer contributions are explicitly opt-in with per-piece resource disclosures.

Losing a required piece cancels the unfinished model task. A replacement can fill the gap and replay it. Cloudflare coordinates and stores outputs; there is no inference fallback to the artist's Mac. Saved weights and records survive an empty habitat. “Alive” describes ongoing computation, not consciousness or irreversible deletion.

## Why this is a statement

A familiar digital pet makes invisible infrastructure tangible. Its apparently individual voice depends on borrowed machines and inherited records. Visitors can see the difference between attention, actual inference, and a claim of intelligence. The meaningful output is a growing cabinet of small creations plus a legible laboratory notebook—not a promise to solve physics.

## Deliberate boundaries and future paths

- No secretly staged escape or real hosting vulnerability. An explicitly fictional, isolated escape chapter remains a future artwork.
- No claim of having processed arXiv, increased intelligence, or supported thousands of simultaneous helpers without doing and measuring it.
- Memory experiments begin with synthetic, exactly scored recall tasks and fixed size budgets. Results are a small, untrusted-browser demonstration, not a general benchmark of agent memory.
- Arbitrary code execution, real paper processing, stronger-model selection and thousands of simultaneous helpers remain alternative/future projects. The shared-layer model and bounded memory workshop are the chosen implementation.
